Most happy customers never leave a review, not because they don't want to, but because nobody asked them at the right moment. If you want to know **how to get Google reviews** steadily, the answer is not a one-week push or a paid service. It is a simple routine your team repeats with every customer. This guide shows the routine we recommend to Kerala businesses, and the shortcuts that can get a profile penalised.

## Why reviews matter more than most owners think

When someone searches "cake shop near me" or "dentist in Kannur", Google shows a map with three businesses. Reviews feed the "prominence" signal Google uses to choose those three, and they are the first thing people read before they call.

Three things matter, not just the star rating:

- **Count:** a 4.7 rating from 60 reviews looks safer than a 5.0 from four.
- **Recency:** reviews from last month tell people you are open and active.
- **Content:** reviews that mention the product, the service or the town ("best birthday cake in Payyanur") help Google understand what you are known for.

## What Google does not allow

Before the routine, the rules. Google's policies for reviews prohibit:

- **Buying reviews**, or paying an agency or "review group" to post them.
- **Incentives:** discounts, free items or lucky draws in exchange for a review.
- **Review gating:** asking only happy customers, or sending unhappy ones to a private form instead.
- **Reviews from staff, family or yourself**, including from other accounts.
- **Review swaps** with other businesses.

Breaking these rules can lead to reviews being removed, a warning on your profile or a suspension. A slow, honest review count is worth far more than a fast one that disappears.

## The review routine, step by step

### 1. Get your review link

In your Google Business Profile, choose "Ask for reviews" (or "Get more reviews") and copy the short link. It opens the review box directly, which removes the biggest point where people give up.

### 2. Ask at the moment of happiness

Ask right after the good moment, not days later:

- **Shops and bakeries:** at billing, or when the customer picks up a custom order.
- **Clinics:** at the end of a successful visit, at the front desk.
- **Service businesses:** when the job is handed over and the customer says thank you.
- **Real estate:** after the site visit that went well, or at handover.

### 3. Use a short WhatsApp message

Most Kerala customers are already chatting with you on WhatsApp. Send one message, the same day:

> Thank you for choosing us today. If you have a minute, a Google review helps other families find us: [your review link]

Keep it personal and short. Do not offer anything in return.

### 4. Put a QR code where people wait

A small QR code at the billing counter, reception desk or on the parcel box lets people review while they wait. Print the review link as a QR code, with one line: "Liked us? Tell others on Google."

### 5. Ask everyone, not just the happy ones

This feels risky, but it is both the rule and the smart choice. A few honest mixed reviews make your profile believable, and the replies you write to them show future customers how you treat people.

### 6. Reply to every review within two days

- **Positive:** thank them by name and mention what they bought or the service, naturally.
- **Negative:** stay calm, apologise for the experience, offer to fix it offline with your phone number, and never argue in public.

Replies are read by future customers as closely as the reviews themselves.

### 7. Track it monthly

Note your review count and rating on the first of every month. A steady rise of even five to ten genuine reviews a month for a local business usually beats competitors who collected 50 reviews once and stopped.

## Common mistakes we see in Kerala

- Asking only once, in a festival rush, and never again.
- A review link buried in a highlight on Instagram where nobody finds it.
- Replying only to complaints, which makes the profile feel defensive.
- Using a different business name or phone number on Google than on the signboard and WhatsApp.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I offer a discount for a Google review?

No. Google does not allow incentives of any kind for reviews, including discounts, freebies or contest entries. Ask customers without offering anything in return.

### Can I delete a bad review?

You cannot delete a review yourself. You can reply to it, and you can report it to Google if it breaks Google's policies, for example if it is spam or from someone who was never a customer.

### How many reviews does a local business need?

There is no fixed number. Compare yourself with the three businesses in the map results for your main search and aim to keep pace with them, with reviews arriving every month.

### Can customers write reviews in Malayalam?

Yes. Reviews in Malayalam are fine and look natural to local customers. Reply in the language the customer used.
